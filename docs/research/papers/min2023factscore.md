## Core method

Argues that a binary factual/not judgement on a long generation is inadequate because generations mix supported and unsupported content. Breaks a generation into a series of **atomic facts** and defines FActScore as the percentage of atomic facts supported by a reliable knowledge source. Reports a large human evaluation of biographies generated by several commercial models, then introduces an automated estimator using retrieval plus a strong LM, reported at under 2% error against the human scores.

## Inputs / outputs

**In:** a long-form generation + a knowledge source. **Out:** a set of atomic facts, each supported/unsupported, and the aggregate percentage.

## Strengths

- **The strongest justification in the literature for claim-level decomposition**, which is the first stage of our pipeline.
- Demonstrates that aggregate percentages hide per-fact detail — our per-claim reporting requirement inherits directly from this.
- Shows an automated estimator can track human judgements closely, which makes the whole approach credible.

## Limitations

- **Binary supported/unsupported.** There is no contradicted class, no unverifiable class and no outdated class, so a fabrication and a true-but-unretrievable fact land in the same bucket. This is the single biggest gap we build on.
- Decomposition is LLM-driven, so it is neither cheap nor fully reproducible.
- Assumes one reliable knowledge source rather than several sources of differing reliability that may disagree. *(verify on full read)*
- Evaluated chiefly on biographies, a domain where Wikipedia coverage is unusually good.

## Compute cost

LLM calls for decomposition plus retrieval and an LM judgement per atomic fact. The paper notes the human equivalent of one of its evaluations would have cost about $26K.

## Reusable for VeriFact

**Our definition of an atomic claim is derived from FActScore's**, and must be cited as such. Our decomposition module is the rules-first, reproducible counterpart of their LLM decomposition.

## Threat to our novelty

**High on decomposition — we claim no novelty there.** Our differentiators are the non-binary status set, the source-reliability weighting across disagreeing sources, calibration, and abstention-aware correction, none of which FActScore addresses.
