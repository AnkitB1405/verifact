## Core method

185,445 claims created by mutating sentences drawn from Wikipedia, then labelled Supported / Refuted / NotEnoughInfo by annotators who did not see the originating sentence (Fleiss κ = 0.6841). For the first two labels the annotators also recorded the specific evidence sentences. A pipeline baseline is provided.

## Inputs / outputs

**In:** a claim. **Out:** a 3-way label + the evidence sentence set.

## Strengths

- **The three-label scheme including NotEnoughInfo is the direct ancestor of our status set**, and it establishes that a serious benchmark refuses to collapse missing evidence into false.
- Gold *evidence* annotations make Recall@k measurable, not just verdict accuracy.
- The reported baselines are sobering and honest: 31.87% for correct label *with* correct evidence versus 50.91% ignoring evidence — which also demonstrates how misleading verdict-only accuracy is.

## Limitations

- Claims are **synthetically mutated**, not naturally generated by an LLM, so the error distribution differs from ours.
- Wikipedia-only evidence: no source disagreement, no reliability differences, no recency dimension. Our outdated and ambiguous categories simply cannot be evaluated on FEVER.
- Known dataset artifacts allowing label prediction without evidence. *(verify on full read)*

## Compute cost

Dataset; the retrieval index over the Wikipedia dump is the real cost.

## Reusable for VeriFact

**Our Recall@k measurements and the retrieval/NLI baselines run on a FEVER dev subset.** Its three labels map into ours (Supported→SUPPORTED, Refuted→CONTRADICTED, NEI→UNVERIFIABLE/INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE) and the mapping table goes in the report.

## Threat to our novelty

None — it is our evaluation substrate. But it also **bounds what we can claim**: the OUTDATED and AMBIGUOUS statuses must be evaluated on our own hand-built set, and the report must say so.
